# 000020 — The charter is three files

- status: accepted
- date: 2026-08-17

## Problem

The charter named two files while three carried the same authority, so the estate had a concept with no single word for it. `AGENTS.md` said *"All three are locked, maintainer-merge-only"* because it had to count what it could not name. The objective said "the charter and constitution" in both boundary clauses and again in the pair's definition. C2's skeleton marked all three maintainer-merge-only one file at a time. One concept — this repository's maintainer-confirmed law — wore two names and an enumeration, which is the duplication C1 exists to end.

The narrow term also made a load-bearing sentence untrue. The objective called the charter *"the pair's whole instruction"*, while the bylaw it contains is *"valid only where it does not conflict"* with a constitution the term excluded. A pair holding only the charter held a subordinate law without the superior law that bounds it, and C1 calls a document asserting what is not so a blocking finding.

## Decision

Maintainer-instructed. The charter is three files: `00-OBJECTIVE.md`, `00-BYLAW.md` and `CONSTITUTION.md`. The first two are co-created with the maintainer in the initiation session; the third is the universal constitution as this repository holds it, by reference. All three are maintainer-merge-only, and that shared authority is the term's whole basis.

Filenames do not move and the `00-` prefix does not spread. The prefix marks provenance — exactly the two artifacts co-created at initiation (000008) — and the charter marks authority. They are different axes, and both are now stated where they belong: the prefix rule in C2's casing grammar, the charter definition in C2's own paragraph beneath it.

The epoch locks the three files as the tree holds them, and the constitution's own text stays live at its one home. Amending a charter is a per-repository act; amending the constitution is one estate-wide act that reaches every charter at once. The objective's after-ship rule carries the same boundary, so a ticket's scope change can never read as licence to amend universal law.

## Options considered

- **A third `00-` file** — rejected; 000008 stands unweakened. The prefix would mark a generated pointer as co-created law, which is the lie that record named. 000018 has since moved four fifths of the provenance ladder out of the co-created bylaw and into the pulled constitution, so the distinction the prefix encodes carries more weight now, not less.
- **A new collective term, "trinity set"** — rejected by the maintainer after argument. The epoch tags `chartered` and `chartered-YYYY-MM-DD-HHMM` are named after the charter and immutable by ruleset, so a rename would orphan the estate's only anchor. The estate's vocabulary is governance-legal throughout, and 000006's test applies unchanged: the governance term encodes the subordination hierarchy the literal name does not, while "trinity" encodes only a count. A number inside a name breaks the moment the set changes. And `trinity` is a Linux system call fuzzer carried in distribution manpages, a grep collision inside the estate's own domain, which the fleet's naming rule forbids.
- **Keep the charter at two and let the enumeration stand** — rejected: it is the C1 violation itself, and it leaves the objective's "whole instruction" claim untrue.
- **Pin the fetch line, so the epoch locks the constitution's text and not only its reference** — rejected on adversarial review taken before this record. A pin makes agents read superseded law by construction rather than by accident, and the bump that would cure staleness is maintainer-merge-only, manufacturing one human act per amendment per consuming repository against the objective's "there is no second approval" and C11's rule against human summons. It would have made the 2026-08-17 incident worse: a revert to a pre-fix charter would have restored the C4 that commissioned the forbidden-channel scan, whereas the live pointer let the estate revert the mess without reverting the lesson. C5 forbids working from a stale pin and C10 demands the live artifact at check-time; a session-start fetch of the one home is what both require. The charter's third member is therefore the reference, and the epoch's coverage is stated as the files rather than the law — so the widened term claims exactly what it delivers.

## Consequences

Swept in the same change, in both repositories. Here: the objective's definition, its after-ship boundary and both merge-authority clauses, which lose the "charter and constitution" doublet; the bylaw's Authority; `AGENTS.md`, whose "All three are locked" enumeration collapses into the term; `CONSTITUTION.md`, which now states its own membership; and `README.md`. In `homelab-root`: C2 gains the charter definition and marks the three in the skeleton, C11's epoch clause names the three law files as the tree holds them, the Authority trail records this record, and the genesis kit carries all of it — the manual's opening, steps 5 and 8 and the surface table, and the `AGENTS`, `00-OBJECTIVE`, `00-BYLAW`, `CONSTITUTION` and `README` templates — so every future repository is born with the widened term.

Frozen records name the charter as the two initiation artifacts: ADRs 000008, 000014 and 000016 through 000019, and the changelog's earlier entries. Read them in their epoch's sense, the device 000005 established for the C/B renumbering and 000019 reused for the dropped prefix.

The charter's membership changed under maintainer confirmation, so C11 calls for a new epoch above `chartered-2026-08-17-2032`, and the floating `chartered` moves onto it — a maintainer act, since the floating name is maintainer-movable by its own ruleset.
